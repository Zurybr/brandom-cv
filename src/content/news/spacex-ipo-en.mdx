On June 12, 2026, SpaceX will begin trading on Nasdaq at a valuation of **$1.77 trillion** — the largest initial public offering in history. Elon Musk becomes the world's first trillionaire. JPMorgan's Jamie Dimon personally pitched the deal to ultra-wealthy clients.

But beneath the headlines, something unprecedented happened in the 48 hours before the bell.

## The Rules Were Rewritten

**Fidelity cut its account minimum by 99.6%** — from $500,000 to $2,000 — days before the offering. The most exclusive door on Wall Street, suddenly wide open to millions of retail investors, right before the biggest debut in market history.

Why would they want *you* inside now?

SpaceX reserved **30% of the operation for retail investors** — three times the normal quota. And even then, most retail participants did not receive their full allocation.

## The Nasdaq Exception

SpaceX will not enter the Nasdaq-100 on day one. It enters **15 days later**, because Nasdaq cut its own waiting period from three months to 15 days — just for this IPO.

When SpaceX joins the index, every QQQ fund on the planet will be **forced to buy**. Estimates range between $22 and $27 billion in automatic purchases.

Imagine 50 buses required to stop at the same gas station on the same morning.

## The Selloff Explained

Two forces are driving the current market selloff:

1. **Retail raising cash** to buy SpaceX on the open market after missing their IPO allocation
2. **Smart money selling ahead** of the forced-index buying in July, front-running the $25B+ avalanche

That is your selloff. Not a crash. A repositioning.

## The Numbers Don't Lie

SpaceX's S-1 filing, submitted May 20, 2026, revealed uncomfortable truths:

- **Revenue**: $18.7 billion (2025)
- **Net income**: **-$4.9 billion** (negative — operating losses)
- **Total equity**: $2.6 billion
- **Valuation**: $1,770 billion

That is a **price-to-sales ratio of ~95x** on a company losing nearly $5 billion a year. For comparison, Amazon at its 1997 IPO had a P/S of ~20x.

Morningstar called SpaceX "significantly overvalued," noting the valuation relies on "novel and untested" technologies.

## The AI Angle

The real catalyst for the IPO timing: compute. In May 2026, Anthropic signed a $1.25 billion/month contract with xAI (SpaceX subsidiary) for Colossus data center capacity. Google followed with $920 million/month. The IPO raises $75 billion to fund orbital AI infrastructure — data centers in space.

SpaceX is no longer just rockets. It is positioning itself as an AI compute provider.

## The Precedent

This pattern has played out twice before:

- **Dot-com 2000**: Insiders exited at absurd valuations while the masses chased hype
- **SPAC mania 2021**: Retail investors were handed the keys to companies that collapsed within 18 months

When the smartest money on the planet builds a $1.7 trillion exit door and hands the keys to the smallest investors in the market, that is not generosity. That is **distribution at the top**.

## Musk's Control

After the IPO, Musk controls **82.4% of voting power** through special shares. Public shareholders have essentially no governance voice. The IPO price of $135/share was set unilaterally, bypassing typical Wall Street price-discovery mechanisms.

## The Bottom Line

You have two options in the next 48 hours:

Chase the most expensive IPO in history at the open, or read the prospectus and realize the exit might be **you**.

The next few days are going to be historic. Just make sure you are on the right side of history.
